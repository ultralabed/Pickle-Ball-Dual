How to Play Pickle Ball Dual: Controls, Shots and Timing

Pickle Ball Dual is played with the keyboard: WASD to move, Space to drive, and J K L for the drop, dink and lob. This guide covers every control, every shot, the foot-ring timing colours, the game modes and the four DUPR difficulty levels.

How do I start a match?

Open the game, press Play, choose Doubles or Singles, a scoring system and the opponents' DUPR level (3.0, 4.0, 5.0 or 6.0), then press Start match. It runs in your browser, with no download and no sign-up.

  1. Open the game and press Play in a desktop browser such as Chrome or Safari.
  2. Choose mode and scoring: Doubles (you and an AI partner against two AI opponents) or Singles, then Traditional scoring (to 11) or Rally scoring (to 15).
  3. Choose the opponent level: DUPR 3.0, 4.0, 5.0 or 6.0 (plus your partner's level in doubles), your paddle hand and graphics quality, then press Start match.
  4. Serve: press Space for a fast deep serve (J or K for a soft serve, L for a lob serve). Hold A or D as you serve to aim left or right.
  5. Return and rally: let the serve bounce before you return it. Move with WASD and press a hit key when the ring under your feet turns green; lime is perfect timing.
  6. Win the game: move up to the kitchen line, use drops and dinks, and attack high balls with a smash. In traditional scoring the first side to 11 wins by 2; in rally scoring the first to 15 wins by 2.

What are the controls in Pickle Ball Dual?

Move with W A S D (or the arrow keys), hit with Space (drive), J (drop), K (dink) and L (lob), and hold A/D (or ←/→) as you hit to aim left or right. The mouse is used for the menus.

What shots can I hit, and when should I use each?

You have four button shots (drive, drop, dink and lob) and two the game triggers for you, the smash and the around-the-post shot (ATP). Use soft shots to take control at the net and hard shots to finish a point.

What is a drive?

A drive is a fast, flat, deep groundstroke, played with Space. Tap for a quick drive or hold to charge more power and release to hit. Use it when the opponents are standing back or out of position, which is common against 3.0 opponents who stay at the baseline.

What is a drop shot (the third-shot drop)?

A drop is a soft arcing shot that lands in the opponent's kitchen, played with J. After the serve and the return have each bounced, the serving side's third shot is usually a drop from the baseline: it gives you time to walk up to the kitchen line, and a ball that bounces in the kitchen cannot easily be attacked.

What is a dink?

A dink is a very soft, low shot that lands in the opponent's kitchen, played with K. Trade dinks patiently from the kitchen line and wait for a high ball to attack. In the game a dink from far back turns into a weak drop, so move up to the net first.

What is a lob?

A lob is a high arc that lands deep near the opponents' baseline, played with L. Use it to push opponents away from the net or to catch them while they are crowding the kitchen line. A lob that lands in for a winner earns a highlight replay.

What is a smash?

A smash is a steep, very fast overhead shot hit downward at a high ball. You do not need a special key: when you press Space and the contact point is above about 1.6 m (5 ft 3 in), the drive automatically becomes a smash. Save it for pop-ups near the net.

What is an ATP shot?

An ATP (around the post) is a shot hit around the outside of the net post instead of over the net. It is legal at any height, even below the net, as long as it lands in the opponent's court. In Pickle Ball Dual it happens automatically: if you reach a ball while you are wide outside the sideline near the net (about 3.5 m from the centre line) and a path around the post exists, your shot becomes a low ATP. 6.0 opponents attempt it too.

How does the foot ring show me when to swing?

A ring under your player's feet changes colour to tell you when to press a hit key: amber means the ball is coming, green means press now and you will make contact, lime means perfect timing, and red means swinging now would be a fault.

Foot ring colours
Ring colourWhat it means
AmberThe ball is coming. It is not time to swing yet, so get ready.
GreenPress a hit key now and you will make contact.
LimePerfect timing: hit now for the steadiest, strongest shot.
RedSwinging now is a fault (a volley before the bounce, or standing in the kitchen).

A yellow circle on the court marks where the ball will land. The paddle meets the ball about 0.17 seconds after you press the key, so press when the ring turns green rather than when the ball is on top of you. Lime gives the steadiest, strongest shot.

What game modes does Pickle Ball Dual have?

You can play doubles (you and an AI partner against two AI opponents) or singles (you against one AI), with traditional side-out scoring to 11 or rally scoring to 15; both are won by 2. Details of each are on the rules page.

  • Doubles or singles: in doubles you also choose your partner's DUPR level.
  • Scoring: traditional (only the serving side scores, to 11) or rally (every rally scores, to 15).
  • Setup options: right- or left-handed paddle, and Low, Medium or High graphics. Your choices are saved in your browser.
  • Instant replays: after a highlight point (a smash, ATP or lob winner, a rally of 10 or more shots, or the point that wins the game) the game replays it in slow motion. Press R or click to skip.
  • Languages: English or Traditional Chinese (繁體中文), switchable from the main menu or Settings.

Does the game call the score like a real referee?

Yes. Before each serve an English referee voice calls the score ("four, two, one", or "zero, zero, two" at the start of a doubles game) and the call is also shown on screen. It also announces side outs, second servers, points, faults and game point. You can turn the voice off in Settings.

What do the DUPR difficulty levels mean?

DUPR (Dynamic Universal Pickleball Rating) is a real-world skill rating that runs from about 2.0 to 8.0. Pickle Ball Dual borrows four levels (3.0, 4.0, 5.0 and 6.0) as presets for how strongly the AI plays: a higher level moves faster, reacts sooner, makes fewer errors and plays smarter.

DUPR difficulty levels
LevelSkillHow the AI plays
3.0BeginnerSlow to move, reacts in roughly 0.45 s and makes plenty of errors. Stays back, mostly drives and lobs, and pops balls up. Good for learning the controls.
4.0IntermediateMedium speed, reacts in roughly 0.30 s. Mixes in third-shot drops, comes up to the kitchen and dinks.
5.0AdvancedFast, reacts in roughly 0.20 s. Reliable drops, patient dinking, attacks high balls and targets your weaker side. Occasionally tries an ATP.
6.0ExpertVery fast, reacts in roughly 0.12 s and rarely misses. Changes pace, speeds the ball up at your body, takes the net and often goes for an ATP.

These are AI presets, not an official rating: playing here does not change anyone's real DUPR. Your own player moves at about the speed of a 4.5 player.

What tips help a beginner win?

Start against 3.0 opponents, let the serve and return bounce, get to the kitchen line, and be patient: most points are won by the side that makes fewer mistakes.

  • Read the hint line on screen: it reminds you of the two-bounce rule and warns you when you are standing in the kitchen.
  • Press the hit key when the ring is green or lime, not before.
  • After your serve is returned, play a drop with J and move forward behind it.
  • Do not volley from inside the kitchen. If the ring is red, let the ball bounce or step back.
  • Aim down the middle (hold no direction key) while you are learning; it is the safe shot.
  • Dink until a high ball appears, then smash it.
