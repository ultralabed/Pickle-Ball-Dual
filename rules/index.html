Pickleball Rules as Played in Pickle Ball Dual

Pickleball is played on a 20 × 44 ft (6.10 × 13.41 m) court over a 34 to 36 in net. The serve and its return must bounce, and nobody may volley inside the 7 ft (2.13 m) kitchen. This page explains each rule as Pickle Ball Dual implements it and says plainly where the game simplifies the official rules.

What are the basic rules of pickleball?

The server hits an underhand serve diagonally into the opposite service court; the serve and the return must each bounce before anyone volleys; nobody may volley while standing in the kitchen; and in traditional scoring only the serving side scores, with games played to 11 points and won by 2.

  • Serve underhand and diagonally, past the kitchen line.
  • Let the serve bounce, and let the return bounce (the two-bounce rule).
  • No volleys in the kitchen (the non-volley zone).
  • A rally ends when a side faults: the ball goes out, into the net, bounces twice, or a rule is broken.
  • First to 11 points, winning by 2 (traditional scoring), or first to 15, winning by 2 (rally scoring in this game).

How big is a pickleball court and how high is the net?

A pickleball court is 20 ft wide and 44 ft long (6.10 m × 13.41 m), the same for singles and doubles. The net is 36 in (0.914 m) high at the posts and 34 in (0.864 m) high at the centre.

All lines are part of the court: a ball that touches a line is in. The one exception is the kitchen line on a serve, which counts as short.

How do you serve in pickleball?

You serve underhand from behind your baseline, diagonally across the net into the opposite service court, and the ball must clear the kitchen line. You get one attempt, and a serve that touches the net but lands in the correct court is simply in play.

  • A serve landing on the sideline, baseline or centreline is good. Landing in the kitchen or on the kitchen line is a short serve, and landing in the wrong court is a fault.
  • You serve from the right half when your side's score is even and from the left half when it is odd.
  • In Pickle Ball Dual press a hit key to serve: Space is a fast deep serve, L a lob serve and J or K a soft serve. The game does not check the paddle height at contact that the official underhand-serve rule requires.

What is the two-bounce rule in pickleball?

The two-bounce rule says the ball must bounce once on each side after the serve before anyone may volley it: the receiving side must let the serve bounce, and the serving side must let the return bounce before hitting its third shot. After those two bounces, volleys are allowed.

It stops the serving team from rushing the net behind a big serve and keeps the opening shots a rally rather than a smash contest. In Pickle Ball Dual, volleying too early is a "Two-bounce violation" fault, the hint line reminds you whenever the rule applies, and the foot ring turns red when swinging would be a fault.

What is the kitchen (non-volley zone) in pickleball?

The kitchen is the 7 ft (2.13 m) zone on each side of the net where you may not volley, meaning hit the ball before it bounces. The kitchen lines belong to the kitchen, so standing on the line counts as standing in it.

  • You may stand in the kitchen at any time, and you may hit a ball that has bounced there.
  • It is a fault to volley while standing in or touching the kitchen.
  • It is also a fault if your momentum after a volley carries you into the kitchen. The game uses a simple version of this: stepping into the kitchen within 0.6 s of a volley is a fault.
  • The kitchen is why dinking exists: a soft ball that bounces in the kitchen cannot be volleyed by an opponent standing there.

What counts as a fault in pickleball?

A fault ends the rally and the side that committed it loses the rally. In Pickle Ball Dual the faults are: the ball landing out, the ball hitting the net and not crossing, bouncing twice on your side, volleying against the two-bounce rule, a kitchen violation, the ball hitting a player's body, and serving short or into the wrong court.

How does traditional (side-out) scoring work in doubles?

In traditional scoring only the serving side can score a point. Each side gets two servers per turn (except the very first turn of the game), and the side loses the serve, called a side out, when both servers have lost a rally. Games are played to 11 and won by 2.

What does 0-0-2 mean in pickleball?

0-0-2 is the score call at the start of a doubles game: the serving side has 0, the receiving side has 0, and the person serving is server number 2. The side that serves first gets only one server in its first turn, so it starts on server 2 and loses the serve after its first lost rally.

What do server 1 and server 2 mean?

The score is called as three numbers: the serving side's points, the receiving side's points, and the server number (1 or 2). Server 1 serves first in each turn; if the serving side loses a rally, server 2, the partner, serves next; if server 2 loses a rally, it is a side out.

  • A turn starts with the player standing in the right court as server 1 (except the opening 0-0-2 serve).
  • When the serving side wins a rally it scores a point, the two partners swap halves, and the same server serves again from the other half.
  • When the serving side loses a rally, server 1 becomes server 2 (the partner serves from the half they are already standing in); if server 2 loses, it is a side out.
  • The receiving side never swaps halves, and the receiver is the opponent diagonally opposite the server.

Example: the game starts at 0-0-2. If the serving side wins the rally, it is 1-0-2. If it loses the rally instead, it is a side out and the other side serves at 0-0-1. If that side then wins a rally, it is 1-0-1; if it then loses a rally, it is 1-0-2 (the partner serves), and a second lost rally hands the serve back.

How does scoring work in singles?

In singles there is one server per turn and only the server scores. You serve from the right half when your score is even and from the left half when it is odd, and the call has two numbers: the server's score, then the receiver's. A lost rally by the server is an immediate side out.

What is rally scoring in pickleball?

In rally scoring every rally scores a point for the side that wins it, whether that side served or not. In Pickle Ball Dual rally games go to 15 points and are won by 2.

  • The serving side serves from the right half when its score is even and from the left half when it is odd.
  • In doubles the players do not swap halves; the player standing on the correct half serves.
  • There is no second server: when the serving side loses a rally, it is a side out.
  • The call has two numbers: the server's score, then the receiver's.

Official rally-scoring formats vary in the real world (games to 15 or 21 are common), so check your event's rules if you play outside the game.

How many points do you need to win a pickleball game?

In traditional scoring a game is played to 11 points and must be won by 2, so 11-9 wins but 11-10 does not and play continues. In Pickle Ball Dual rally scoring is played to 15, also win by 2. Real tournaments sometimes use 15 or 21 points.

Which official rules does Pickle Ball Dual simplify?

The game follows the core rules but simplifies a few details to suit a keyboard game against the AI. If you are learning for real play, remember these differences.

  • Kitchen momentum: the real rule is about momentum carrying you into the kitchen after a volley; the game uses a 0.6 s window.
  • Not modelled: end changes, time-outs, touching the net, double hits and the paddle position on a serve.
  • Serve return: returning a serve with the receiver's partner is not penalised.
  • Net cord: a ball that clips the net and carries on is live for any shot.
  • Rally scoring: the game plays to 15, win by 2; real formats vary.
